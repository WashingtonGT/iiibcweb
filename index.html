IIIBC DIGEST — NOVEMBER 2026 — ISSUE 18
FOR: Chief AI Officers and AI Strategy Leaders
─── THIS WEEK'S SIGNAL ────────────────────────

On January 1, 2027, California's CCPA automated-decision rules and Colorado's rewritten AI Act (SB 26-189) start applying to AI used in hiring, lending, housing, and other significant decisions about people. Colorado enforcement is stayed in federal court; the duties are not. If a tool screens résumés, scores applicants, or routes service decisions, you need pre-use notice, an adverse-decision explanation, and a human-review path before New Year's Day. Waiting for federal preemption is not a plan: Congress has passed nothing.

─── NOISE TO IGNORE ───────────────────────────

"AI is driving a mass-layoff wave." Challenger's AI-attributed US job cuts peaked at 38,579 in May and fell to 1,265 in October, under 3 percent of the month's total. Those figures record what employers claim, not what caused the cuts. The real story is quieter: employment of 22–25-year-olds in AI-exposed roles is 19 percent below trend because companies stopped hiring juniors (Stanford, August). Cut the junior pipeline now and you will have no senior bench in 2029.

─── CAIO DECISION THIS WEEK ───────────────────

Inventory every AI system and agent that makes or shapes a decision about a person, or acts without human approval. For each one, record a named owner, notice language, a human-review path, a spend cap, and a kill switch. Four hours with your HR, operations, and engineering leads. Skip it and you enter January with undisclosed automated decisions in two states, and with agents nobody can switch off. Deloitte's October 2026 survey of 3,500 executives found that about 40 percent of firms already let AI act without human approval.

─── ONE SENTENCE FOR YOUR BOARD ───────────────

"New California and Colorado rules cover every AI tool that screens or scores people from January 1. We will have an inventory, notices, and a named owner for each tool in place before then."
