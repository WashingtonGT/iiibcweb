Before you go

Six things to settle before wheels-up. The first two — your timed national-park tickets — are the ones that quietly make or break a peak-summer trip.

01 Book Plitvice early

Peak-summer entry is timed and capped — tickets routinely sell out 3–4 weeks ahead. Buy a morning Entrance 1 slot on the official site np-plitvicka-jezera.hr (avoid lookalike resellers). ~€40/adult; ~55-minute arrival window.

02 Book Krka too

Your Day 5 second park is also timed and capped in August. Reserve an early slot on the official npkrka.hr and enter via Skradin (scenic boat shuttle in) or Lozovac. Going early beats both the midday heat and the crowds at Skradinski Buk.

03 One country, one car

The whole route stays inside Croatia — no Montenegro, no Green Card, no border queues. At the Alamo counter in Zagreb, just confirm the one-way drop at Dubrovnik (DBV) is on the contract.

04 The Pelješac Bridge

On the Day 6 Split→Dubrovnik drive, make sure your nav routes you over the Pelješac Bridge. It leaps the Neum corridor so you stay in Croatia the whole way — no Bosnia crossing, no extra paperwork.

05 One biometric check-in

Croatia is in Schengen, so your only border formality is arrival at Zagreb airport, where the EU's biometric EES registers you on first entry. Pre-load the "Travel to Europe" app to speed it up. The euro is used throughout — keep a little cash for konobas and parking.

06 Two festival nights

Two evenings are worth booking ahead: the Split Summer Festival in Diocletian's Palace (Aug 9) and the Dubrovnik Summer Festival inside the old-town walls (Aug 11). Both stage opera, concerts and theatre in spectacular historic settings.

Thursday · Aug 6 · Arrival

Landing in Zagreb

Drive ~25 min from ZAGStay Sheraton ZagrebLand 10:40 AM
The Upper Town · Gornji Grad

Medieval Zagreb on the hill

Collect the car, drop bags at the Sheraton, then climb into the Upper Town for your first afternoon. This is the oldest, most atmospheric quarter — cobbles, gas lamps still lit by hand each evening, and the city's postcard landmark, St. Mark's Church. Its dazzling 1880 roof tiles spell out two coats of arms: the medieval kingdoms of Croatia, Dalmatia and Slavonia on the left, the city of Zagreb on the right.

Fun fact The church square is flanked by the Croatian Parliament and the prime minister's office, so it's often fenced and the church itself usually opens only around Mass — but the roof, the real star, is best admired from outside anyway.
Reopened April 2026

Zagreb Cathedral

Croatia's tallest building and the grandest Gothic structure south-east of the Alps reopened to visitors in April 2026 after six years of repairs following the 2020 earthquake. Step inside (open daily ~08:00–19:00); expect some scaffolding still wrapping the twin 108-metre spires.

  • Ride the funicular — at 66 metres, one of the world's shortest public railways, running since 1890 — up to the Lotrščak Tower.
  • Be near the Lotrščak Tower at noon: the Grič cannon has fired a single daily blast since 1877, a tradition said to keep the city's clocks in sync.
  • Wander Tkalčićeva Street — once a millstream, now the city's liveliest run of cafés and restaurants — and settle in for dinner.

Friday · Aug 7 · Zagreb

Museums, market & a hill town

Optional drive ~45 min to SamoborStay Sheraton Zagreb
A museum morning

Zagreb's small, brilliant collections

With a full day, lean into the city's museums. The Museum of Broken Relationships is unlike anything else — donated objects and the stories of the love affairs they outlived; it won a European Museum of the Year award and spawned imitators worldwide. Pair it with the Mimara Museum and the Museum of Arts & Crafts for old masters and design.

Fun fact "The belly of Zagreb," the Dolac market, has traded under its sea of red parasols since 1930 — a working farmers' market right above the main square, perfect for a mid-morning browse.
Afternoon escape

Samobor — or Varaždin

For an easy outing, Samobor (~45 min) is a postcard hill town whose café tradition revolves around the samoborska kremšnita, a towering vanilla custard slice. If you'd rather have more architecture, swap in Varaždin (~1 hr), a beautifully preserved Baroque town once Croatia's capital, optionally with hilltop Trakošćan Castle and its lakeside walk.

Saturday · Aug 8 · To the coast

Plitvice Lakes, then Split

Drive ~2 hr to Plitvicethen ~2.5 hr to SplitStay AC Hotel Split (night 1)
UNESCO World Heritage · since 1979

Why the water is that colour

Plitvice is Croatia's oldest and largest national park, a chain of sixteen terraced lakes that spill into one another over natural travertine dams. Those barriers are alive: mosses and bacteria deposit limestone (tufa) that grows by roughly a centimetre a year, slowly raising the dams and re-sculpting the lakes. The unreal blues and greens come from the mineral-rich water, the lake-bed, and the angle of the light — which means the colours shift hour by hour.

Fun fact The forests around the lakes still shelter brown bears, wolves and lynx. And in the 1960s these waterfalls doubled as the American "Wild West" in the hugely popular German Winnetou films.
Plan it as the day's main event

Walking the boardwalks

This is not a quick photo stop. Treat your booked morning slot seriously, start at Entrance 1 for the dramatic Lower Lakes and the 78-metre Veliki Slap (Croatia's tallest waterfall), and budget around four hours including the electric boat and panoramic shuttle. Then drive down to the coast — you'll reach Split in the early evening.

  • Wear grippy shoes — the boardwalks get slick with mist and have no railings.
  • Check in to the high-rise AC Hotel Split for sweeping views over the city and the sea.
Tonight, if you have the energy

Šotobraco Splitom — a folk-dance promenade

On select August evenings the FA Jedinstvo ensemble dances its way from the National Theatre through the old town to the Riva (free, around 7:30 PM). It's a no-commitment, walk-along bit of living tradition — ideal for a tired arrival night. The 2025 dates were Aug 8/14/18/28; confirm the 2026 calendar at visitsplit.com.

04
Sunday · Aug 9 · Split

Inside a Roman palace

On foot all dayStay AC Hotel Split (night 2)Festival night ★
Built ~305 AD · still inhabited

A palace that became a city

Split grew up inside the retirement palace of Diocletian — the Roman emperor famous for being the only one to voluntarily abdicate the throne. Sixteen centuries later, thousands of people still live and work within the old palace walls: laundry hangs between Roman columns, cafés spill across the ceremonial Peristyle, and shops occupy the imperial substructures. It's not a ruin behind a rope; it's a living neighbourhood.

Fun fact A black granite sphinx Diocletian shipped from Egypt — already ~1,500 years old when it arrived — still sits in the Peristyle, roughly 3,500 years old today. And the emperor who persecuted Christians now lies beneath a building reborn as the Cathedral of St. Domnius, among the oldest cathedrals still in use anywhere.
  • Climb the cathedral bell tower (built atop Diocletian's mausoleum) and explore the cavernous substructures — the same cellars that played Daenerys's throne room in Game of Thrones.
  • Stroll the palm-lined Riva waterfront, then hike forested Marjan Hill, the "lungs of Split," for a sunset over the islands.
  • Half-day option that suits you perfectly: drive 15 minutes to Klis Fortress — a clifftop stronghold that held off the Ottomans (and stood in as GoT's Meereen) — paired with the sprawling Roman ruins of Salona next door.
★ Tonight — your headline performance

Split Summer Festival, in the Peristyle

The 72nd Splitsko ljeto (Jul 14–Aug 14) stages opera, ballet and concerts in the open-air Roman courtyard at the heart of the palace — music inside seventeen centuries of stone. Book the Aug 9 night as soon as the programme posts at splitsko-ljeto.hr.

Monday · Aug 10 · The added day

Krka, then Šibenik in stone

Drive ~1 hr to KrkaŠibenik ~20 min onStay AC Hotel Split (night 3)
National park no. 2 · a flatter, swimmable cousin

Krka & Skradinski Buk

Your extra Split night buys a second waterfall park — and a gentler one. The Krka river drops over the same kind of living travertine dams as Plitvice, but its showpiece, Skradinski Buk, is a single broad curtain you circle on a flat boardwalk loop in well under two hours. Arrive early via Skradin, riding the scenic park boat upriver to the falls.

Fun fact In 1895 the falls powered the Jaruga hydroelectric plant — one of the world's first AC power stations, switched on just two days after the Niagara plant. The little town of Šibenik downstream had public electric street lighting before most of Europe's capitals.
UNESCO World Heritage · 20 min from Krka

Šibenik & the Cathedral of St. James

Exactly your kind of stop: a stone old town climbing to a hilltop fort. The 15th–16th-century Cathedral of St. James is built entirely of stone — no brick, no timber, no mortar binding — its great dome and barrel vault assembled from interlocking slabs, a feat of engineering unique in Europe. Then climb St. Michael's Fortress above town for the short, steep view you like (it doubles as an open-air summer stage).

Fun fact Around the cathedral's outer apse runs a frieze of 71 sculpted heads — not saints but ordinary 15th-century townsfolk, every one a different face, said to be the people who did (or didn't) help pay for the church.
  • Pick the earliest Krka slot on npkrka.hr — it beats the heat and the day-tripper crush at the falls.
  • Back in Split for the evening: an easy Riva dinner, or a second Split Summer Festival night if the Aug 10 programme tempts you (the festival runs through Aug 14).
Optional tonight

A second night of the Split Summer Festival

You're still in Split, and Splitsko ljeto runs through Aug 14 — so if the Aug 10 programme has something you like, it's an easy bonus evening in the Peristyle with nothing to organise but a ticket. Otherwise, a relaxed seafood dinner on the Riva is the perfect close to a park day. Programme at splitsko-ljeto.hr.

Tuesday · Aug 11 · Down the coast

The Pelješac road to Dubrovnik

Drive ~3–3.5 hr via the bridgeStay Hilton Imperial DubrovnikWalls & festival ★
Morning · the great coastal drive

South over the Pelješac Bridge

Check out mid-morning and run south down the Makarska Riviera, mountains on one side and islands strewn across a hard blue sea on the other. The drive's headline act is the Pelješac Bridge: a 2.4-km cable-stayed span, opened in 2022, that vaults the Neum corridor so you reach Dubrovnik entirely within Croatia — no Bosnia crossing, no passports at all today.

Worth a stop Where the bridge meets the mainland, Ston guards a 5-km chain of medieval defensive walls — the longest in Europe after China's — and farms the oysters that have been prized here since Roman times. A plate of them by the water is the ideal mid-drive lunch.
Afternoon & evening · the Pearl of the Adriatic

Dubrovnik's walls at golden hour

Check into the Hilton Imperial Dubrovnik, two minutes from the Pile Gate, and walk straight into the old town. Stroll the marble Stradun, then walk the City Walls — a ~2-km circuit past the round Minčeta Tower and out over Fort Lovrijenac. Go late afternoon as the heat eases and the stone turns gold; for an aerial finish, ride the Mount Srđ cable car above the rooftops at sunset.

Fun fact Dubrovnik was the merchant Republic of Ragusa, a tiny sea power that rivalled Venice, abolished the slave trade in 1416, and whose never-breached walls more recently played King's Landing in Game of Thrones.
  • Leave the car at the hotel — the old town is car-free and entirely walkable from the Pile Gate.
  • Buy the City Walls ticket (also covers Fort Lovrijenac) and start the circuit anticlockwise to keep the best light ahead of you.
★ Tonight — your final headline performance

Dubrovnik Summer Festival, inside the walls

For 45 summer nights (~Jul 10–Aug 25) the Dubrovnik Summer Festival turns the old town's courtyards, churches and forts into stages — classical concerts, opera, drama and dance under the open sky. A performance within the ramparts is the ideal last-night anchor. Book the Aug 11 programme at dubrovnik-festival.hr.

Wednesday · Aug 12 · Departure

A last morning in Dubrovnik

Airport ~30 minCar back by 10:00 AMFly DBV 12:15 PM
The easy exit

No border, no rush

Because the trip ends in Croatia, the white-knuckle border morning is gone: Dubrovnik airport is only ~30 minutes from the hotel. If your 12:15 United flight leaves time, slip into the old town for an early Stradun stroll before the cruise crowds land — the marble streets are at their loveliest empty — then collect anything you missed on the walls.

One last view The cliff-edge Buža bar, reached through a hole in the seaward wall, serves a morning coffee on the rocks straight above the Adriatic — a quiet, perfect full stop before you point the car at the airport.
  • Have a relaxed breakfast at the hotel, then drive ~30 min and return the car at Dubrovnik (DBV) by 10:00.
  • Top up the tank near the airport on the way in to avoid the rental refuelling fee.

The Adriatic Crossing

Seven days down the Croatian Adriatic — from a Roman emperor's palace to the walls of Dubrovnik, with two waterfall parks, stone old towns and a great deal of good seafood in between. Drive safely, eat well, and book the festival nights early.
